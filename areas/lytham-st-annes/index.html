Lytham St Annes

Home Care in Lytham St Annes

Support at home in Lytham, St Annes-on-the-Sea, Ansdell and Fairhaven.

In short

  • We support people in their own homes across the FY8 postcode district.
  • Adult social care here is arranged through Lancashire County Council.
  • Many people here arrange and pay for care privately, and we can talk this through.
  • Call us for a free home assessment.

Who arranges care in Lytham St Annes

Lytham St Annes is in the Borough of Fylde, and adult social care is provided by Lancashire County Council. Anyone can ask the council for a care needs assessment, and a separate financial assessment decides how much you contribute. You can start at Lancashire County Council. Our guide to who pays for home care explains each route in plain English.

Where we support people

We cover the FY8 postcode district, including Lytham, St Annes-on-the-Sea, Ansdell and Fairhaven. Lytham St Annes is a popular place to retire, and many of the people we expect to support live in bungalows, retirement apartments or their own family homes. If you live in a retirement development, ask the management about access and any rules for visiting carers, because that can affect how we plan visits.

Hospital care and coming home

Hospital care for people in Lytham St Annes is mostly provided at Blackpool Victoria Hospital. If you are about to be discharged, ask the ward early about the plan. Our guide to coming home from hospital sets out the questions to ask, and professionals can find our referral information.

Living near the coast

Coastal homes can be more exposed in winter weather, and some people prefer to keep their routines even when it is cold or wet. We plan visits with this in mind and agree with you how to deal with things like keeping the house warm and making sure the doorstep is safe.

Questions about care in Lytham St Annes

Which council do I contact about care in Lytham St Annes?
Lancashire County Council arranges adult social care here. Anyone can ask for a care needs assessment.
Can care be arranged privately without involving the council?
Yes. Many people choose to arrange and pay for care directly with an agency, whether or not they also ask the council for an assessment.
