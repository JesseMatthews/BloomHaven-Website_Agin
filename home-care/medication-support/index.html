Health

Medication support at home

Taking the right medicine at the right time matters. We help you do it safely.

Many people forget doses, find packaging difficult or take several medicines at different times. Mistakes can lead to illness or hospital admission. Help can range from a reminder to a carer giving medicines that are prescribed and dispensed for you.

What this can include

  • Reminders and prompting, so that you take your own medicines
  • Support to take medicines that are prescribed for you, from a pharmacy-labelled pack or blister pack, where this is agreed in your care plan
  • Clear written records of what was taken and when
  • Telling you, your family or your GP when a dose is missed or something seems wrong
  • Help with collecting prescriptions, arranging deliveries and storing medicines safely
  • Noticing changes that may be side effects, and passing them on

Who it helps

  • People with several medicines or complicated timings
  • People with memory problems who may forget or double up
  • People who have just left hospital and have new medicines

How it works

Your care plan says exactly what help is agreed. Where medicines are involved we follow NICE guidance on managing medicines for adults receiving social care in the community (NG67). Carers are trained and assessed as competent before they support with medicines. Records are kept for every visit.

Things to bear in mind

Carers cannot change doses or give medicines that have not been prescribed to the person. They also cannot give injections or other clinical tasks unless this is arranged through the right healthcare professional and agreed in advance. If something is outside what we can safely do, we will say so.

Questions

Can carers give any medicine?
No. Carers support with medicines that are prescribed to the person, as set out in the care plan. Anything outside this must be arranged with a healthcare professional.
What if a dose is missed?
We record it and contact the right person, such as family, the GP or pharmacist, as set out in the care plan.
