Wellbeing

Companionship and social support

A friendly, reliable person to talk to, go out with and share the day.

Loneliness is bad for health and mood. For some people the most useful help is not personal care at all, but regular company from someone they like and trust. Companionship visits can be an hour a week or a few times a day.

What this can include

  • Conversation, cups of tea and shared interests
  • Accompanying you to appointments, shops, clubs and social events
  • Hobbies: gardening, crosswords, cards, music, reading aloud
  • Help to stay in touch with family, including by phone and video call
  • A walk or trip to somewhere you enjoy
  • Help with letters, post and simple everyday tasks

Who it helps

  • People living alone who would like regular contact
  • People who have lost a partner or friends
  • People who no longer feel confident going out alone
  • Families who live far away and want to know someone is looking in

How it works

We take time to match you with someone you get on with. Many people begin with companionship and add other support later, when it is needed. It is a sensible way to build trust before personal care starts.

Things to bear in mind

Companionship carers are not nurses and cannot give clinical care. If your needs change, we will talk to you about what else might help.

Questions

Can the carer drive me?
Carers can accompany you on outings and appointments, including by public transport or taxi. Please ask us about driving arrangements and insurance.
Can I have the same person each time?
We aim for continuity and will always try to send someone you know.
