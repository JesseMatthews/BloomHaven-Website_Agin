Arranging care for a parent from a distance

It is hard to help when you cannot be there. These steps help you make good decisions with your parent, and keep a clear view of how they are doing.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 2 minute read

In short

  • Start with a conversation. Your parent’s wishes come first.
  • Ask their council for a care needs assessment, and consider lasting power of attorney.
  • Pick one point of contact, and agree what information is shared and with whom.
  • Plan regular check-ins, and ask for updates and reviews in writing.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

Begin by listening

Ask your parent what they find hard, what they want to keep doing, and what worries them. People often fear losing independence more than they fear needing help. If they have capacity to decide, the choices are theirs, and it is your job to support them.

Get an assessment

Ask their local council for a care needs assessment. You can usually start the process for them, with their agreement. See who pays for home care for how this works in Lancashire and Blackpool. If you are not nearby, ask whether the assessment can be arranged at a time you can join by phone.

Legal and practical paperwork

  • Lasting power of attorney for health and welfare, and for property and finance, is best arranged while your parent can make the decision. See GOV.UK
  • Make sure the GP surgery and key services know who your point of contact is
  • Keep a shared list of medicines, appointments, key contacts and house information

Choosing and managing care from afar

  • Use our ten questions and ask any agency to explain things by phone or video
  • Ask who your contact is, and how you will hear about changes or concerns
  • Ask for the care plan in writing, and a date for the first review
  • Think about a key safe, so that carers can get in safely

Staying in touch

  • Regular phone or video calls at times that suit your parent
  • A trusted neighbour or friend who can look in and tell you if something changes
  • Visits that include time to look around, not only time to talk
  • Agree with the agency what you would like to be told, and when

Looking after yourself

Caring at a distance carries its own stress, including guilt. You do not have to do it all. Asking for help, from the council, from friends, or from an agency, is part of doing it well.

Questions

What if my parent says they do not need help?
Go gently. Ask about specific things that have become difficult. A small first step, such as a few hours of company or help with shopping, can build trust without feeling like a big change.
Can I speak to the agency on my parent’s behalf?
Usually with your parent’s permission, yes. A good agency will agree with your parent what information is shared with you.
