How to choose a home care agency: 10 questions to ask

Choosing who comes into your home, or a parent’s home, is a big decision. These questions help you compare agencies fairly and spot the ones worth trusting.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 2 minute read

In short

  • Check the agency is registered with the CQC for personal care, and read its latest report.
  • Ask about consistency: will you have regular carers, and what happens when they are off?
  • Get the price, and what is and is not included, in writing before you agree.
  • Ask how complaints are handled before you ever need to make one.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

Start with registration

In England, personal care at home can only be provided by a provider registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC). Search for the agency on the CQC website and check that the location is registered for “personal care”, then read the most recent inspection report and note its date. A new agency may not have been inspected yet, and a good one will tell you that plainly. See also what registration status means.

Ten questions worth asking

  1. Are you registered with the CQC, and can I see your latest report? A good answer gives you a name or registration number you can check yourself.
  2. Who will actually visit me? Ask whether you will have a small group of regular carers and whether you can meet them first.
  3. What happens if my carer is ill or on holiday? Cover should be planned, not improvised.
  4. How long are visits, and are they ever cut short? Ask whether travel time is built in, so the visit is not squeezed.
  5. What does it cost, and what is extra? Ask about evenings, weekends, bank holidays and two carers at once.
  6. How are carers recruited and trained? Look for enhanced DBS checks, references, induction and ongoing training.
  7. How do you write and review the care plan? It should be written with you, and reviewed regularly and when things change.
  8. Who do I call, and when? Ask about office hours and what happens out of hours or in an emergency.
  9. How do you handle complaints? You should get a clear process, a timescale and a named route to take it further.
  10. What notice do I need to give to change or stop care? Read any contract before you sign it.

Signs of a good agency

  • They ask about you, not just your tasks, and write down what matters to you
  • They are open about what they cannot do, and about gaps in cover
  • Prices and terms are in writing and easy to follow
  • You can speak to a real person, not only a call centre

Warning signs

  • Pressure to sign quickly
  • No clear answer about CQC registration
  • Vague answers about who will visit
  • Costs that are hard to pin down

Involve the person receiving care

If the person has the capacity to decide, the choice is theirs. Include them in meetings, and ask the agency to explain things directly to them, not only to family.

Questions

Should I use an agency or employ a carer directly?
Both are legitimate. An agency handles recruitment checks, cover and management for you. Employing directly gives more control, but you become an employer and take on responsibilities such as tax, insurance and arranging cover.
Can I change agency if it is not working?
Yes. Check the notice period in your agreement, and tell the agency what is not working first, because many problems can be fixed.
