Coming home from hospital: arranging care at home

Discharge can happen quickly, and it can feel overwhelming. A little preparation, and the right questions, make coming home safer and calmer.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 2 minute read

In short

  • Talk to the ward about discharge plans as early as you can, not on the day.
  • Ask whether short-term support after discharge is available to you.
  • Check what equipment, medicines and follow-up are in place before you leave.
  • Plan the first week: who visits, who to call, and when the plan will be reviewed.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

Ask about discharge early

Hospitals plan discharge with the person, their family and a team that can include nurses, therapists and social workers. Ask the ward what the plan is and who is responsible for it as soon as you can. In this area, many people are treated at Royal Preston Hospital or Blackpool Victoria Hospital, and each has a discharge team you can ask to speak to.

Short-term support after discharge

Some people are offered short-term support, often called reablement or intermediate care, to help them regain independence at home. This is commonly provided free for a limited period, often up to six weeks, but what is offered depends on your needs and local arrangements. Ask the discharge team whether it applies to you and what happens when it ends.

Questions to ask before you leave

  • Has an assessment of my needs been done, and can I have a copy of the plan?
  • What medicines am I going home with, and who explains them?
  • Is any equipment needed, such as a bed, commode or walking aid, and who is arranging it?
  • Who will check on me in the first few days, and how do I contact them?
  • Is there follow-up from the GP, district nurses or therapists?
  • Who do I call if things are not working?

If you are arranging care yourself

You can arrange care privately before or after discharge. Contact agencies early, tell them the likely discharge date and the type of support needed, and ask honestly whether they can start in time. Ask for a written plan and price, as in our guide to choosing an agency. Please note that we cannot take on personal care until we are registered with the CQC. See our registration status.

The first week at home

  • Make sure food, heating and medicines are in place before you arrive
  • Have a key safe or agreed way for carers and professionals to get in
  • Keep a list of contacts by the phone
  • Ask for the care plan to be reviewed early, because needs often change in the first week or two

Questions

Can I refuse to be discharged?
You have the right to be involved in decisions about your care. If you feel a discharge is unsafe, say so clearly to the ward manager and ask for a review. Advocacy services and the hospital’s patient advice team can support you.
Does the NHS arrange care after discharge?
Sometimes. Some people receive short-term NHS or council-arranged support, and some need long-term care arranged separately. Ask the discharge team what applies to you.
