Starting dementia care at home: where to begin

Seeing someone you love change is hard, and the first steps can feel unclear. This guide covers practical places to begin, and where to find expert support.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 2 minute read

In short

  • If you are worried about memory, start with the GP. Many things can cause memory problems.
  • Consistent carers and familiar routines help people with dementia the most.
  • Good dementia care is about the person, their history and what they enjoy, not only tasks.
  • Look after yourself too. Support exists for carers and families.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

Start with the GP

Memory problems have many possible causes, and some can be treated. A GP can check for these and refer for a diagnosis if needed. A diagnosis can open the door to support, benefits and planning ahead, and it helps carers understand what to expect.

Expert support to know about

Organisations such as the Alzheimer’s Society and Dementia UK offer advice lines, information and support for people living with dementia and their families. They are good places to turn at any stage.

What good dementia care at home looks like

  • Consistency: a small team of familiar carers, so that faces and routines become reassuring
  • Knowing the person: their life story, likes, dislikes and what calms or upsets them
  • Patience and time: not rushing, and offering choices in simple ways
  • Routines: regular mealtimes, activities and sleep patterns
  • Safety, without taking away independence: a careful balance, reviewed as things change
  • Family involved: kept informed and listened to

Practical things to put in place

  • Think about lasting power of attorney while the person can still decide who they trust
  • Make the home safer: lighting, trip hazards, locks and medicines
  • Keep important information together: medicines, appointments, contacts
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment, and for a carer’s assessment for yourself

Looking after yourself

Family carers often put themselves last. Regular breaks, support groups and sharing the load make caring more sustainable, for you and for the person you support. Respite care, including a few hours of home care, can give you time to rest. Learn more about our approach to dementia care.

Questions

When is the right time to bring in help?
Earlier than most families expect. Starting with a few hours a week lets the person get to know a carer while things are calmer, and makes it easier to increase support later.
What if they refuse care?
This is common and understandable. Introducing help gradually, as a companion rather than as “care”, and involving people they trust, often helps. Respect their wishes where they have capacity to decide.
