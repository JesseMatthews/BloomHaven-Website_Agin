Who pays for home care in Lancashire and Blackpool?

Funding depends on your needs and your finances, and on which council covers your address. Here is how to find out where you stand.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 3 minute read

In short

  • Anyone can ask their council for a care needs assessment, whatever their finances.
  • Lancashire County Council covers most of Lancashire. Blackpool Council covers Blackpool.
  • A separate financial assessment decides how much you pay.
  • NHS Continuing Healthcare and Attendance Allowance are different routes, worth asking about.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

Which council do I ask?

Adult social care is not run by the same council across the area we work in. Lancashire County Council is responsible for adult social care in places such as Preston, Lytham St Annes and Fleetwood. Blackpool Council is a separate unitary authority and is responsible in Blackpool. If you are unsure, check the council tax bill for your address. You can start at Lancashire County Council or Blackpool Council.

Step 1: a care needs assessment

Anyone who may need care can ask their council for an assessment of their needs. This is free and does not depend on your income or savings. It looks at what you find difficult and what would help. You can find the national guidance on GOV.UK.

Step 2: a financial assessment

If the council is going to help arrange or pay for your care, it will look at your income, savings and some assets to decide how much you contribute. The limits and rules are set nationally and can change, so ask the council for the current figures rather than relying on old ones. People above the limit usually pay for their own care, and many choose to arrange it directly.

Direct payments

If the council funds some or all of your care, you can often ask for a direct payment: money paid to you so that you can arrange care yourself, including through an agency of your choice. Ask the council whether this is available for you.

NHS Continuing Healthcare

If you have a significant ongoing health need, the NHS may fund your care in full through NHS Continuing Healthcare. It is not means-tested. Eligibility is assessed against national criteria, usually starting with a checklist. A GP, nurse or hospital team can start this, and the NHS social care and support guide explains the process.

Benefits that may help

Attendance Allowance is for people over State Pension age who need help with personal care or supervision because of illness or disability. It is not means-tested and does not depend on you receiving care. Carers may also be able to claim support in their own right, so it is worth checking.

Getting independent advice

Funding is complicated, and mistakes can be costly. Age UK and your local council can explain your options, and a regulated financial adviser who specialises in care costs can help with long-term decisions. We are not financial advisers, but we are happy to explain what our own care would cost, in writing, after an assessment.

Questions

Do I have to pay if I own my home?
It depends on the type of care and the rules in force. The value of your home is treated differently for care at home than for a permanent move into a care home. Ask the council for the current rules for your situation.
Can I ask for an assessment on behalf of someone else?
Yes, family members and friends can ask for an assessment for someone else, though the person should be involved and their wishes respected.
