Live-in care or a care home: how to decide

Neither is better for everyone. The right answer depends on the person’s needs, wishes, health and home. This guide sets out the differences honestly.

By the Bloomhaven Care team · Last reviewed 18 November 2026 · 2 minute read

In short

  • Live-in care keeps someone in their own home with one carer living with them.
  • A care home provides a shared setting, with staff on site at all times.
  • The right choice depends on needs, wishes, safety and cost compared in full.
  • Ask the person what matters most to them, and involve them throughout.
General information only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Rules and services change, so please check current details with the organisations we link to.

What each option is

Live-in care means a carer lives in the person’s home, usually working in shifts over a period of days or weeks, with breaks and rotations arranged by the agency. A care home provides a room, meals, support and staff on site, either with personal care or with nursing, in a shared building.

When live-in care often works well

  • The person wants to stay at home, and the home can be made safe
  • They need help throughout the day or at night, but not constant nursing care
  • Couples want to stay together, which a single room rarely allows
  • Pets and routines are important, and part of what makes home feel like home

When a care home may be the better fit

  • Needs are complex or nursing care is required around the clock
  • The home cannot be adapted, or there is no spare room for a carer
  • Isolation is a concern, and company and activities matter
  • Care at home cannot be arranged safely or reliably

Questions to ask yourself and your family

  • What does the person themselves want?
  • What are the risks of staying at home, and can they be managed?
  • Is there a suitable room, and can the family accept a stranger living in the home?
  • What would the total cost be for each option, including everything extra?

Not all or nothing

Some people start with visiting care and move to live-in care later. Others try respite stays in a care home, or use live-in care to recover after hospital. Needs change, so the decision can be revisited. You can read more about our approach to live-in care.

Questions

Is live-in care cheaper than a care home?
It depends. For one person with moderate needs, a care home is often less expensive. For a couple, or someone who needs a lot of support, live-in care can compare well. Always compare the full cost of each, including everything that is extra.
Does the carer get time off?
Yes. Live-in carers are entitled to breaks and rest, and a good agency plans cover so that the person is never left without support.
