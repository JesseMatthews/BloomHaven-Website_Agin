Costs and funding

Paying for home care

A plain-English guide to how care is funded, and the questions to ask before you commit.

In short

  • You can pay for care yourself, or the council or NHS may help. It depends on your needs and finances.
  • Anyone can ask the council for a care needs assessment, whatever their finances.
  • You will always get a written quote from us before you agree to anything.
General information only. Funding rules and thresholds change. Please check current figures with your council, GOV.UK or an independent adviser such as Age UK. We are not financial advisers.

Four common routes

1. Paying for it yourself

Many people arrange and pay for care directly. You choose the provider, agree the plan and pay the agency. You can do this even if the council would pay part of the cost.

2. Council support

Anyone can ask their local council for a care needs assessment. A financial assessment then decides whether you pay all, some or none of the cost. In Preston, Lytham St Annes and Fleetwood this is Lancashire County Council; in Blackpool it is Blackpool Council.

3. NHS Continuing Healthcare

For people with significant ongoing health needs, the NHS may fund care in full. Ask your GP or hospital team about a CHC checklist. More information is on the NHS social care and support guide.

4. Benefits

Benefits such as Attendance Allowance can help towards costs and are not means-tested.

What affects the price

  • How many visits you need and how long they are
  • Whether care is needed at night, at weekends or on bank holidays
  • Whether you need two carers at once, for example for moving and handling
  • Live-in care costs more than visiting care but covers all hours

Questions to ask any provider

  • Are you registered with the CQC, and can I see your latest report?
  • What exactly is included in the price, and what is extra?
  • Will I have a regular carer, and what happens if they are off?
  • How do you handle complaints, and who do I speak to?
  • What notice is needed to change or end care?

Questions

Does the council pay for home care?
Sometimes. The council will assess your care needs and, separately, your finances. If your savings and assets are above the national limit, you will normally pay for your own care. If you are below it, the council may contribute. Everyone can ask for a care needs assessment, regardless of income.
What is NHS Continuing Healthcare?
NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC) is a package of care that the NHS funds for people with a primary health need that meets certain criteria. It is not means-tested. A health professional can start the assessment process.
What is Attendance Allowance?
Attendance Allowance is a benefit for people over State Pension age who have a disability or illness that means they need help with personal care or supervision. It is not means-tested and you can claim whether or not you receive care.
Will you give me a price in writing?
Yes. After your assessment we will give you a written quote that explains the visits, hours and rates, and any extra costs, before you agree to anything.
